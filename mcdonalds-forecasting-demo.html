Common Forecasting Challenges
  • Reactive, not predictive: Spreadsheet-based 4-week averages and manager gut-feel adjustments can't anticipate sudden demand shifts — weather events, promo launches, or TikTok trends.
  • 20-minute UHC holding rule: Cooked nuggets and patties in the Universal Holding Cabinet must be discarded after 20 minutes. Over-batch leads to direct waste. Under-batch leads to customer wait times.
  • 48–72 hour ordering lead time: Martin-Brower orders can't be corrected same-day. Emergency off-cycle shipments incur steep freight penalties.
  • Limited freezer capacity: Walk-in freezers hold only 3–4 days of inventory. Over-forecasting causes congestion; under-forecasting leads to stockouts within 48 hours.
  • Invisible cannibalization: When a Chicken Big Mac LTO launches, McChicken drops ~15% and McNuggets drop ~8% — spreadsheets don't model cross-product effects.

This is a real improvement — but it only reacts to what's already in the data. It can't tell you why Tuesday looked odd, or catch a supply shortfall before it hits the counter.

What ML Handles Well
  • Day-of-week demand curves (Friday peak, Monday dip)
  • Seasonal patterns (summer chicken sandwich lift)
  • Weather-demand correlation (rain/cold = +10% comfort food)
  • Holiday effects (day-before surge, day-of dip)
  • Known promotion lift (scheduled campaigns)
  • Product mix trend shifts over time
  • Regional baseline differences
What ML Still Misses
  • Unplanned promotion extensions or surprise menu additions
  • Competitor actions (Chick-fil-A opens nearby)
  • Social media demand surges (TikTok hack goes viral)
  • Supply chain disruptions (supplier delay, truck rerouting)
  • Real-time intra-day demand shifts
  • Cross-product cannibalization from new launches
